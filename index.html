What makes a great wine & spirits shop

Four things separate a destination shop from a beer-and-lotto counter.

  1. Selection with a point of view. Allocated bourbon, single-barrel store picks, grower Champagne, natural and kosher wine, agricole and Jamaican rum, mezcal beyond the one smoky bottle — depth signals a real buyer behind the shelf.
  2. Service that knows the inventory. Staff who bridge you from what you drink to what you'll love — and who call you when an allocated bottle lands.
  3. Fair, transparent pricing. Independents with lower overhead often beat big-box shelf prices. Always compare; don't assume the chain is cheaper.
  4. Storage and turnover. Wine kept cool and moving, not baking under fluorescent lights.

How to find the hard-to-find bottles

Three categories, three different games.

Bourbon

Allocated & rare

"Allocated" means rationed to shops in small quantities. Land it by becoming a regular (shops offer it to regulars first), chasing single-barrel store picks at retail pricing, and entering Florida's ABC Vault lottery. Read the label — mash bill, proof, age — not the hype.

Wine

Hard-to-find & natural

Natural wine is a minimal-intervention philosophy, not a certification; pét-nat is finished by the méthode ancestrale. Kosher wine has climbed in quality — look for mevushal for catered events. Bordeaux is blended and classified; Burgundy is single-varietal, by tiny climats.

Spirits

Rare craft

Additive-free tequila rejects the up-to-1% of undisclosed additives the rules allow — verify a brand independently. Mezcal spans 30-plus agaves across nine states. Rum rewards nerds: agricole vs. molasses, Jamaican high-ester "funk," cask strength.

Questions, answered

How do I find allocated bourbon in Florida?

Allocated bourbon is sold through independent shops — which offer it to regular customers first — and Florida's ABC Vault lottery. Building a relationship with a shop and asking about single-barrel store picks is the most reliable route.

What does "additive-free" tequila mean?

Producers may legally add up to 1% of undisclosed additives (glycerin, caramel color, oak extract, sweeteners). Additive-free tequila uses none. Verify a brand against an independent source, since labels don't always disclose additives.

Is natural wine the same as organic wine?

No. Organic is certified farming; natural wine is a broader, uncertified philosophy of minimal intervention — native yeasts, little or no added sulfur, minimal filtering.

Are independent shops more expensive than the big chains?

Often the opposite — family-run shops with lower overhead frequently beat big-box prices on the same bottle. Compare before assuming the chain is cheaper.
