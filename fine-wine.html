Fine Wine in Florida, County by County

Florida has quietly become one of America's serious wine markets — no state excise on the collector's mind, year-round entertaining, and cellars from Palm Beach to Naples. Here's how to buy fine wine anywhere in the state, what separates a real merchant from a shelf of labels, and our pick for the deepest cellar in South Florida.

Researched and published by the team at Primo Liquors, the South Florida retailer featured in this guide. Our recommendation below is our own; other shops are noted for reference, not ranked.

How to judge a fine-wine merchant

  1. Storage you can feel. Fine wine dies warm. A serious shop keeps its good bottles temperature-controlled — if the Burgundy is stacked by a sunny window, walk out.
  2. Provenance on older bottles. Where did that 2005 Bordeaux live for twenty years? A real merchant can answer — source, storage history, fill level, label condition.
  3. Depth in the classics. Anyone can stock 90-point Napa. The tell is the hard stuff: real Burgundy under and over $100, grower Champagne, mature Barolo, First Growths priced honestly.
  4. Allocation access. The wines everyone wants — cult Napa, top domaines — flow to shops with buying power and distributor relationships. Ask what allocations they land.
  5. People who taste. Staff should tell you why a bottle earned its shelf space, not read you the shelf talker. One good question exposes everything: "what would you drink tonight at $40?"

Why 2026 is a buyer's year

Two facts collectors should hold at once: California is producing less wine than at any point in 25 years and growers are pulling vineyards — while current market pricing is the softest in a decade, with distributors clearing slow stock. Supply is tightening before prices have caught up. Well-stored bottles from the labels you already love, bought from a licensed retailer at today's prices, is the value play of the moment.
