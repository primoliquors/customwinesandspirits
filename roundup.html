Weekly roundup · By Dan Denson

This Week in Drinks

Industry news, curated — with our take on what it actually means for people buying bottles. Updated every Monday.

Week of August 17, 2026

Treasury Wine just wrote down $394 million in U.S. assets — DAOU, Frank Family, and BV took the hit

Treasury Wine Estates, the company behind DAOU, Frank Family Vineyards, and Beaulieu Vineyard, flagged another big writedown tied to U.S. oversupply — on top of a writedown it already booked against the same business back in December. Part of the fix is deliberately shrinking future harvests in Napa and Sonoma by fallowing vineyards. These are real, widely stocked labels, and a producer this size cutting production on purpose is usually a two-part signal: aggressive pricing on what's in the market now, and tighter supply on these three names once the smaller harvests work through in a couple of years. via The Drinks Business

1800 Tequila's newest bottle costs $11,000 — and it's not really about the tequila

The brand's Colección Tequila series added Mono Laberinto Doble, a 50-piece run of extra añejo aged in new American oak and casks that previously held Amarone, housed in a nickel decanter designed by the late Mexican artist Pedro Friedeberg. At that price and that scarcity, this is a collector object priced for the design work, not something built for drinking. Worth knowing mostly as a marker of how far a mainstream tequila brand will now stretch upmarket — not something a typical buyer needs to chase. via The Spirits Business

Balcones' founder has a new mezcal, and unlike most buzzy agave launches, you can actually find it

Chip Tate, who built Balcones into one of Texas's best-known craft whiskey names before leaving the company, has a new project: Penumbra, a single-palenque espadín mezcal distilled in Oaxaca for Foley Family Wines & Spirits. It's bottled at 46% ABV and priced at $45, and it's rolling out nationally rather than launching as a tiny allocation. A credible distiller's name plus real national distribution at a normal price is a rarer combination than it should be in mezcal right now — worth putting on the list. via Fred Minnick

Beyoncé now owns SirDavis outright — LVMH is out

LVMH sold its stake in SirDavis, the American whisky Beyoncé launched with the luxury group in 2024, giving her sole control of the brand. The whisky itself hasn't changed — still distilled by MGP in Indiana from a 51% rye, 49% malted barley mash, then finished and bottled in Texas — but losing a distribution giant like LVMH is the kind of ownership shift that can affect how aggressively a brand gets pushed into new stores. If SirDavis is on your shelf now, it's worth watching whether availability holds steady as the brand rebuilds its distribution relationships on its own. via The Drinks Business

Week of August 10, 2026

Bordeaux's wildfires missed the big vineyards — but the harvest timing still matters

Wildfires burned roughly 42,000 hectares in the Gironde and forced mass evacuations, but the major appellations — Médoc, Saint-Émilion, Pomerol — weren't directly hit, and growers are heading into an early harvest expecting minimal smoke taint. Smoke damage doesn't always show up until after fermentation, though, so the 2026 vintage won't really be gradeable until well after harvest wraps up in September. Not a reason to skip 2026 Bordeaux, but a reason to wait for actual reviews before buying futures on faith. via Wine Enthusiast

Champagne just capped the harvest at its second-lowest level this century

The Comité Champagne set this year's yield at 8,800 kg per hectare — about 250 million bottles, the tightest cap since the pandemic-shortened 2020 harvest and the fourth straight year of cuts, driven by frost, heat, and drought. Tighter supply out of Champagne now typically shows up as higher prices and thinner allocations two to three years out, once this fruit becomes finished wine. If there's a house you buy every holiday season, don't assume it stays the same price indefinitely. via Wine-Searcher

Silicon Valley Bank says wine's "rock bottom" is still a year or two away

SVB's 2026 wine industry report shows U.S. case volume down to 329 million in 2025 (from 410 million in 2019) and sales value down to $74.3 billion, with the bank's own analysts projecting the real bottom lands in 2027 or 2028. For buyers, the down market has mostly meant better deals and more aggressive discounting as wineries and retailers try to move inventory — that dynamic looks likely to continue for at least another year or two rather than snap back. via VinePair

Tequila's glut is real — agave prices crashed roughly 90% in three years

Agave that sold for around $1.60 a kilogram a few years ago is now going for about $0.10, the result of growers overplanting during the tequila boom; distillers are reportedly sitting on hundreds of millions of liters they can't move. For buyers, an oversupplied category usually shows up as promotional pricing on mainstream blanco and reposado, so this is a reasonable window to trade up a tier without paying a premium for scarcity that isn't really there. via VinePair

More Americans now use marijuana daily than drink alcohol daily

A federal substance-use survey found 21.4 million Americans use cannabis daily or near-daily versus 17.2 million who drink alcohol that often — the first time cannabis use has passed alcohol on a daily basis, with the shift sharpest among Gen Z. Alcohol is still used by far more people overall, so this isn't a five-alarm story, but it's one more data point behind the low/no-alcohol and smaller-format sections that keep expanding in stores — stocking a few credible options there is standard shelf planning now, not a trend bet. via Forbes

Week of August 3, 2026

Canadian alcohol gets a 50% tariff on August 19 — if you drink Canadian whisky, this is your two-week warning

The White House announced a 50% tariff on Canadian goods including alcohol, effective August 19, after several provinces pulled American products from their shelves. Bottles already in the U.S. aren't taxed retroactively, but replacement stock will be, and retailers price off replacement cost. If Crown Royal, Canadian Club, or a favorite Canadian rye is your daily pour, buying ahead this month is the rational move — and watch for knock-on effects, since the broader trade fight touches tequila and bourbon supply chains too. via Park Street

American wine sales head below 300 million cases — and the shrinking shelf cuts both ways

Impact Databank projects U.S. wine volume down 3.5% this year, under 300 million cases for the first time in over 15 years — the fifth straight annual decline. For a buyer, a shrinking category means two things at once: the labels still growing (Josh, La Marca Prosecco, Decoy, Mionetto) will stay easy to find, while everything else becomes a deal opportunity as distributors clear slow stock. If you have no loyalty to a label, the discount bins are about to get interesting. via Shanken News Daily

California is tearing out vines — today's wine glut won't last forever

California is producing less wine than at any point in the last 25 years, and major growers are still removing vineyards — Beckstoffer alone plans to pull more than half its vines in Lake and Mendocino counties. The current market is a buyer's market, but supply corrections eventually become price corrections. If there's a California producer you love, this is a good year to buy deep while the category is still oversupplied. via Wine Industry Advisor

The cooler door is eating the liquor shelf

Control-state numbers through June tell a blunt story: traditional spirits down 2.3%, while ready-to-drink cocktails carry all the growth — Cutwater up over 400%, Surfside up 46%, High Noon still the volume king. Florida is not a control state, but the consumer trend is national. Expect your local store's cooler section to keep expanding, and expect the interesting news in cans, not just bottles, for the rest of the summer. via Shanken News Daily

Espolòn is quietly winning tequila — and there's a new Extra Añejo

In a tequila category growing under 2%, Campari's Espolòn grew 8.2% globally and is up 18.5% by volume in control states this year, helped by a new Extra Añejo expression and smaller formats. The market has decided Espolòn is the value play in agave, and the new Extra Añejo brings that reputation to the top shelf. Worth a look before the price finds its audience. via Shanken News Daily

What actually hit shelves this week

Four launches worth knowing from the last week of July: Old Forester's Triple Char bourbon (57.5% ABV, $64.99 per 375 mL, Kentucky-only for now), Bib & Tucker's first bottled-in-bond — an 8-year Tennessee high-rye at $64.99 rolling out nationally in September, Old Elk's three 10-year whiskeys at $69.99 nationwide, and Glenglassaugh 15 (a PX-sherry-matured Highland malt, around $114). The Old Elk trio is the one you can actually walk in and buy; the Old Forester is the one to ask a traveling friend about. via Global Drinks Intel
